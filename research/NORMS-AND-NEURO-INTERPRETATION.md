# Normative Values and Neurological Interpretation of Static Balance Measures

**Purpose.** Reference layer for the Bertec BP5046 balance station (static plate, Fz/Mx/My → COP) with camera-based joint tracking: honest age/sex-referenced comparison, plus a screening report in a clinician's voice — assistive, never diagnostic.

**Status.** Literature synthesis, 2026-10. Every number is quoted from a cited source. Where no norm exists for a band the entry says **not published**. Nothing is interpolated or modelled.

---

## 0. Five rules that govern everything below

1. **Norms are device-bound.** COP path length and velocity depend on plate size, sampling rate, filtering, duration, foot position, footwear and target distance. A Bertec BP5046 value cannot be scored against a NeuroCom, BTrackS, Zebris or Leonardo percentile [34][38][39]. Cross-device comparison is *contextual only* and must be labelled as such.
2. **Duration changes path length, not velocity.** Never compare a 20 s path length (BTrackS) with 30 s or 60 s values without converting to mm/s.
3. **Path length ≠ area ≠ stability.** High velocity inside a small area can be a *good* sign (fast, small corrections); the two dissociate and must be reported together [2].
4. **Sex effects are real but small and inconsistent.** Strong in BTrackS (n=6280) and a Chinese pressure-plate sample (n=1526) [1][6]; small in Leonardo (partial η² < 0.02) and absent except one area measure in a Zebris 18–30 sample [2][5]. Adjust sex only where the source stratified by sex.
5. **A plate produces screening signals, not diagnoses.** No pattern below establishes a lesion, a diagnosis, a fall, or fitness to return to sport.

### 1.3 What the large datasets show about age

- **Era 2006, n = 7,979 (Finland, ages ≥30).** Decline is detectable in young and middle age on force-plate measures, accelerates from ~60; men sway more, gap widens with age; separate sex-specific norms required [35].
- **Leonardo, n = 570 (20–86).** Area and path are **U-shaped** — worst at 20–40 *and* 60–86; **velocity rises linearly** with age. "Area increases monotonically with age" is false [5].
- **Chinese pressure plate, n = 1,526 (20–80).** Males sway more at every age and eye condition; decline begins 40–49 in men, 50–59 in women; EC always worse; height/weight/BMI weak or uncorrelated [6]. Numeric table paywalled — **not extracted**.
- **Meta-analysis, 44 studies.** Bands 50–59, 60–69, 70–79 significantly less stable than 20–29 in EO and EC; females outperform males in EC [34].

SDs are large (≈9–14 s in young bands): treat as distributions, not targets. Reliability: ICC 0.994 (EO), 0.998 (EC). Age-specific and **not related to sex** in this dataset [7]. Ceiling effect 45 s; floor effect ≥80.

Single-leg velocity is roughly 3–4× double-leg velocity on comparable hardware — never score it against double-leg norms. **Left/right-specific norms: not published.** Report each limb, the difference and the ratio; no validated asymmetry cut-off exists for healthy adults.

### 2.3 Fall-history cut-offs from single-leg COP (n=170, mean 67 y, 50 fallers) [9]

| Measure | Cut-off | Performance |
|---|---|---|
| COP area | > 10.30 cm² (F > 7.8; M > 10.8) | AUC 0.72 |
| AP velocity | > 2.93 cm/s | 78% sensitivity (best single) |
| ML velocity | > 3.40 cm/s (F > 3.4; M > 3.2) | — |
| Time to failure | 17 s (fallers) vs 18 s (non-fallers) | **not discriminative** (n.s.) |

Note the tension: hold time did not separate fallers while COP did. They are different constructs; report both.

## 3. mCTSIB (4 conditions, 30 s, sway velocity in °/s)

| Condition | 40–59 y controls (n=29) | 60–69 y controls (n=26) |
|---|---|---|
| Firm, eyes open | 0.27 ± 0.12 | 0.28 ± 0.12 |
| Firm, eyes closed | 0.33 ± 0.13 | 0.31 ± 0.11 |
| Foam, eyes open | 0.63 ± 0.17 | 0.69 ± 0.15 |
| Foam, eyes closed | 1.61 ± 0.45 | 1.60 ± 0.52 |

Source: healthy age-matched controls in a 2025 SBMA study [10]. **Caution:** the two bands are nearly identical, contradicting the larger age-gradient literature [5][34][35]; n=26–29 healthy, possibly active adults. Use as order-of-magnitude anchors, not a normative curve. Paediatric values for the same protocol are far higher (firm EO 0.70 ± 0.32 °/s) and must never be applied to adults [42].

**Measurement error (i-mCTSIB, VSR Sport plate, 20 adults 65–85 y):** ICC 0.762–0.909, composite 0.898. MDC90 = 0.140–0.285 °/s for firm and foam-EO, but **1.12 °/s for foam eyes-closed** — the hardest condition is 4–8× noisier, so small foam-EC changes are uninterpretable [11].

**BTrackS mCTSIB** reports path length in cm, not velocity; decline appears in vision/vestibular (foam) conditions from the 30–39 band while standard/proprioceptive conditions hold until 50–59 [43]. Not transferable to a Bertec plate.

## 4. Age and sex effects — what to model, what to ignore

| Effect | Direction | Magnitude | Practical rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Age, velocity | rises ~linearly from the 30s, steeper after 60 | large (partial η² 0.1–0.4) [5] | always age-reference velocity |
| Age, area/path | U-shaped: elevated 20–40 and 60–86 | large [5] | never assume monotonic decline |
| Sex | females sway less, especially EC | small-to-moderate; strong [1][6], negligible [2][5][34] | adjust only where source stratified |
| Height / BMI | weak or no correlation | small [3][5][6] | do not body-size-normalise linear COP; use angular measures (deg) across heights [33] |
| Athletes vs general | athletes sway less, mainly EC and single-leg; elite < sub-elite | moderate, meta-analytic [36][37] | an athlete at the general-population 50th percentile is *not* reassuring; use sportive young reference [2] and within-athlete change |
| Sport type | sport-specific (dancers better EO, worse EC) | varies [36][37] | never rank athletes across sports |

**Meaningful change vs noise** — MDC/mean is the practical yardstick:

| Measure | ICC | MDC | % of mean | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-leg total velocity | 0.82 | 9.73 mm/s | ~28% | [8] |
| Single-leg AP / ML velocity | 0.85 / 0.76 | 6.26 / 7.08 mm/s | ~29 / 31% | [8] |
| Single-leg AP amplitude | 0.86 | 1.69 mm | ~34% | [8] |
| Single-leg area | 0.71 | 1,466.7 mm² | ~50% | [8] |
| i-mCTSIB firm / foam EO | 0.76–0.91 | 0.14–0.285 °/s | ~40–45% | [11] |
| Bertec SOT composite | — | 6.5 points | — | [23] |

**Consequence:** any within-person change smaller than these MDCs must be reported as "no measurable change". Area is the noisiest metric — demote it to a supporting plot.

## 5. Pattern interpretation (Romberg and friends)

### 5.1 Romberg quotient — weaker than textbooks imply

| Construct | Value in health | Caveat | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| RQ = EC / EO (area or velocity) | ≈ 2.0–2.5 | depends on target distance | [12] |
| RQ at 40 cm fixation | ≈ 2 | near target raises RQ via vergence | [12] |
| RQ at 200 cm fixation | ≈ 1 | far target collapses the ratio; a "normal" RQ of 1 may be a testing artefact | [12] |
| RQ ≤ 1 ("postural blindness") | abnormal | vision neither helps nor is used | 2025 [13] |
| RQ AP range, fall screening | cut-off 1.64 | single-fall screening, ≥65 y | 2017 [14] |
| RQ AP velocity, clinical cut-off | 2.29 (ROC 1.83) | 50% sens / 74.5% spec — poor alone | [14] |
| RQ as vestibular/cerebellar discriminator | **failed** | "did not reliably differentiate patients from controls or the two patient groups" | 1998 [15] |

**Rule:** compute RQ only when both conditions used the same target distance, stance width and duration. Otherwise report the two raw values and omit the ratio.

### 5.2 Screening patterns — plate + video

| Pattern | Plate signature | Video / clinical correlate | Conventional label | What it *may* suggest | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exaggerated Romberg | EO near-normal; EC markedly worse; worse on **firm** EC than foam EC | steady with vision, sways when occluded | sensory / proprioceptive pattern | peripheral neuropathy, dorsal column disease, severe somatosensory loss | a lesion or its level |
| Vestibular pattern | EO and firm EC near-normal; disproportionate worsening on **foam EC**; high foam-EC/firm-EC ratio | falls toward one side on foam, not firm | vestibular pattern | unilateral or bilateral vestibulopathy | which ear, or that vertigo is vestibular |
| Cerebellar / truncal ataxia | high sway in **both** EO and EC; little visual benefit; ML prominence; tandem impossible | wide base, trunk oscillation, cannot narrow stance | ataxia | cerebellar or afferent ataxia; SARA stance grades 2–6 | a specific cerebellar or neurodegenerative disease |
| Wide-based stance | large ML excursion, low velocity; feet far apart | stance width > hip width, arms out | compensatory widening | compensation for instability of any cause (82% of orthostatic tremor patients) | a diagnosis; widening can *normalise* the COP |
| Hip strategy | ML-dominant sway, power shifted > 3 Hz, "stiffening" | hip/pelvis motion, little ankle motion | proximal strategy | ageing, ankle weakness, neuropathy, fear of falling | that the strategy is pathological |
| Weight-bearing asymmetry | mean Fz split outside 43–57% | pelvis shift, unequal knee loading | asymmetry | stroke, pain, unilateral weakness, habit | cause; WBA explains only r² ≈ 0.18–0.25 of sway variance in stroke [21c] |
| Single-leg deficit, unilateral | hold time or COP worse on one side | pelvic drop, trunk lean, arm counterbalance | unilateral impairment | ankle instability, hip abductor weakness, vestibular asymmetry | which structure |
| Test failure / step | trial aborted | stepping, grabbing, opening eyes | inability to complete | fall-risk signal | a diagnosis |

Sources: Romberg proprioceptive/vestibular dissociation [16][17]; cerebellar and SARA stance grading [18][19]; hip vs ankle strategy and ML frequency content [20]; weight-bearing asymmetry [21][21b][21c].

### 5.3 Screening signal vs diagnosis — the line to hold

- **Screening signal:** "sway exceeds the age/sex reference in eyes-closed conditions, with a vestibular-type foam/firm dissociation." A description of behaviour.
- **Diagnosis:** requires history, neuro-otological exam, audiometry, VNG/vHIT, nerve conduction, imaging. Posturography satisfies *no* diagnostic criterion on its own [15][16][17].
- A normal plate never excludes disease; an abnormal plate never confirms it.

## 6. Concussion / mTBI and return to play

| Finding | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Recent concussion, bilateral stance BESS: sway area | 1,500 mm² (95% CI 900–2,000) vs 650 mm² (520–780), p = 0.02 | 2017 [27] |
| Instrumented BESS, adolescents 1 month post-concussion | larger 95% ellipse, AP and ML velocity than controls **while BESS scores were normal** | 2017 [29] |
| Paediatric mTBI, single-leg soft-surface path length | 3,542 ± 1,219 mm (patients) vs 2,380 ± 539 mm (controls) | 2025 [28] |
| Ellipse area in that paediatric cohort | **no** significant between-group difference | 2025 [28] |
| COP-detected deficits after concussion | reported to 15 days, and in football players at RTP clearance (~4 weeks) | [29] |
| Clinical BESS recovery | typically day 3–5 | [29][30] |
| BESS test-retest reliability | ICC 0.50–0.75 (moderate) | [30] |
| mBESS in youth | ICC −0.309 to 0.651 — poor to moderate | 2024 [31] |

**Limits a static plate cannot cross.** It cannot diagnose concussion — SCAT6/SCOAT6 state that no component, including balance, is diagnostic, and a normal score does not exclude concussion [22][32]. It cannot clear an athlete: return to sport is a graduated 6-stage process, ≥24 h per stage, with medical clearance, and no balance metric appears in the criteria [22][32]. It cannot separate vestibular from cervical from oculomotor contributions. It cannot distinguish "still injured" from deconditioned, sleep-deprived, anxious or in pain. Serial BESS-style testing has practice/learning effects; force-plate protocols are more resistant but not immune [29][30].

**Defensible report language:** "Static sway exceeds the age/sex reference in eyes-closed and foam conditions, consistent with the balance domain of a post-concussive presentation. This is a screening finding; it does not establish or exclude concussion and does not inform return-to-play decisions."

## 7. Fall-risk thresholds in older adults

| Measure | Threshold | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unipedal stance time | < 5 s | increased risk of *injurious* fall | [24] |
| Unipedal stance time | < 15 s | increased risk of *repetitive* falls, middle/older adults | 2025 [25] |
| Single-leg COP area | > 10.3 cm² | fall history, AUC 0.72 | [9] |
| Single-leg AP velocity | > 2.93 cm/s | 78% sensitivity | [9] |
| mCTSIB condition II (firm EC) failure | OR 3.56 (1.01–12.57) | future fall, BLSA n=952, 60–97 y | 2025 [26] |
| Single-leg stance failure | OR 1.54 (1.15–2.06) | " | [26] |
| Semi-tandem failure | OR 2.59 (1.51–4.46) | " | [26] |
| RQ of AP range | 1.64 | single-fall screening | [14] |
| Eyes-closed AP velocity | 17.41 mm/s (clinical), 13.78 (ROC, 83% sens) | faller classification | [14] |
| AP velocity, eyes open | 14.24 mm/s | "balance alteration", OR 2.94 | 2021 [44] |

**Do not** convert any of these into an individual probability of falling. They are group-level discriminators at 50–83% sensitivity and 44–89% specificity; predictive values depend entirely on base rate.

**Athletes.** No fall-risk threshold applies. The frame is within-athlete change and comparison with sportive young adults [2][36]; a "high sway" athlete may simply be a poor single-leg balancer with no pathology.

## 8. Presenting "within / outside age-sex reference" honestly

| Band | Percentile | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Below reference | < 5th | "better than 95% of the reference sample" |
| Low-normal | 5–25th | "within the lower part of the reference range" |
| Typical | 25–75th | "typical for age and sex" |
| High-normal | 75–95th | "within the upper part of the reference range" |
| Above reference | > 95th | "outside the reference range (worse than 95% of the reference sample)" |

**Implementation rules**

1. Match on device class, duration, sampling rate, filter, stance width, footwear, vision condition, target distance. If any differ → "contextual comparison, not a percentile".
2. Prefer percentiles over z-scores: COP distributions are right-skewed, so a z implies normality the data do not have. If a z is shown, state the assumption.
3. Show the reference source and n beside every band. "Age-matched" without a citation is not a norm.
4. Never say "abnormal" — say "outside the age/sex reference range".
5. Never aggregate a composite "balance score" across incommensurable devices or conditions.
6. Report raw value + unit + condition in the same cell; unit drift (mm/s vs cm/s vs °/s) is the commonest source of false referrals.
7. State MDC beside any longitudinal comparison [8][11][23].
8. For < 18 or > 80, most static-plate norms do not exist — print raw values and say "no age-matched reference available for this band".

## 9. Report blueprint — what a clinician expects

**Structure (one page, front-loaded):**

1. **Header.** Subject ID, date, age, sex, height, mass, protocol version, device serial, sampling rate, filter, duration, footwear, stance width, visual target distance, trial count, operator.
2. **Referral question.** One line: what the test was ordered to answer.
3. **Impression (≤5 lines, plain language).** Within/outside reference; which domain (sensory, motor, asymmetry); confidence; whether referral is suggested. No jargon, no diagnosis.
4. **Condition table.** Value, unit, age/sex percentile band, reference source, flag. One row per condition; no collapsing.
5. **Sensory ratios.** Romberg quotient and, if foam was used, foam-EC/firm-EC ratio, each with target distance and a note that RQ is distance-dependent [12][17].
6. **Symmetry.** Weight-bearing split (%BW L/R), single-leg hold time L/R, single-leg COP L/R and ratio. Norms: 43–57% WBA [21]; no L/R COP norm — report the difference only.
7. **Strategy.** AP vs ML ratio, ML share, spectral shift above 3 Hz if available, plus the video correlate (ankle vs hip vs stepping) [20].
8. **Functional context.** SARA stance grade if ataxia is suspected [18]; mBESS/SCAT6 domain if concussion [22]; unipedal stance time vs Springer band [7].
9. **Plots** (below).
10. **Limitations.** Device-specific norms, single session, effort/attention, vision correction, footwear, medication, time of day, no diagnostic claim.
11. **Recommendations.** Retest interval (≥ MDC), referral *if* clinically indicated, or "no action". Never a treatment prescription.
12. **Appendix.** Metric definitions (path length, 95% ellipse, RMS, velocity), filter and artefact handling, software version, reference-table version.

**Plots a clinician will look for**

- Stabilogram: AP and ML COP vs time, both conditions overlaid, artefact/step events annotated.
- 95% confidence ellipse with AP/ML axes and area, drawn to scale, EO vs EC side by side.
- Condition bar chart with the age/sex reference **band** shaded behind each bar (not a single line).
- Power spectrum 0.05–5 Hz, AP and ML, EO vs EC — visual signature of stiffening / hip strategy.
- Single-leg: hold time per limb against the Springer age band, plus a COP quality panel.
- Symmetry: L/R weight-bearing bar with the 43–57% corridor, and L/R single-leg comparison.
- Longitudinal panel (if repeat): change vs MDC as a band, not a p-value.

**Tone.** Write as a clinician to a clinician: measured, specific, hedged where evidence is thin, explicit about what was *not* tested. One sentence of uncertainty is worth three of confidence.

## 10. Explicit "do not claim" list

1. Do not claim a diagnosis of vestibular loss, neuropathy, cerebellar disease, Parkinsonism, concussion or anything else from plate or video data.
2. Do not claim fall risk for an individual from any cut-off in §7.
3. Do not clear — or refuse to clear — an athlete for return to play [22][32].
4. Do not transfer a percentile across devices: Bertec ≠ NeuroCom ≠ BTrackS ≠ Zebris ≠ Leonardo ≠ Wii Board. Bertec's own CDP norms differ significantly from NeuroCom on SOT conditions 1, 4, 6 and on the Adaptation Test [23][40].
5. Do not compare path lengths recorded for different durations without converting to velocity.
6. Do not treat sway area as a stability index on its own; it dissociates from velocity [2].
7. Do not assume sway rises monotonically with age — area is U-shaped [5].
8. Do not apply adult mCTSIB values to children, or paediatric values to adults [42].
9. Do not report an RQ computed across different target distances or stance widths [12].
10. Do not claim left/right single-leg norms exist for healthy adults — they do not.
11. Do not present a change smaller than the MDC as improvement or decline [8][11].
12. Do not describe a wide-based stance as "better balance" — widening can normalise COP while masking instability.
13. Do not use "within normal limits" as a synonym for "no disease".
14. Do not silently mix 90% and 95% ellipse definitions — state which [2][5].
